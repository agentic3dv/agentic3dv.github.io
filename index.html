Introduction

Agentic systems are among the fastest-growing directions in AI. This momentum has now reached 3D, where recent agentic systems have shown strong capability in solving 3D reconstruction, setting up simulation environments, and editing 3D graphics. Despite this progress, we still have a limited understanding of why these systems work so well and which questions matter most next. To build that understanding, we will gather researchers in computer vision, computer graphics, multimodal large language models, and robotics to discuss the following topics:

  • Agentic 3D reconstruction and inverse graphics How should agents be designed to recover the geometry, appearance, and motion of a scene from images and videos, and how do they compare with feed-forward methods?
  • Agentic sim-to-real and real-to-sim How can agents build simulation-ready digital twins from real observations, and can they identify the physical parameters needed to close the gap between simulated and real worlds?
  • Agentic 3D editing and generation Should agents edit and create 3D objects and scenes by writing code, operating modeling software, composing generative models, or combining all three?
  • 3D representations and tools for agents Which 3D representations, such as meshes, programs, procedural graphs, and neural fields, make 3D tasks tractable for agents, and what tool interfaces do agents need?
  • Spatial reasoning of VLM agents How well do current agents reason about geometry and physics, and where do they fail?
  • Benchmarks and evaluation What tasks and protocols can measure the capabilities of 3D agents while remaining hard to saturate or game?
  • Efficiency and reliability Can agentic pipelines become cheap and trustworthy enough for practical use, and what role will output verification, feedback, and reinforcement learning play?

Call for papers

The call for papers will open soon. We invite submissions on agentic systems for 3D tasks, including every topic listed in the introduction.

  • We accept short papers of up to 4 pages and long papers of up to 8 pages, excluding references, in the CVPR format.
  • Papers are non-archival and will not appear in the proceedings, so authors can later submit them to archival venues.
  • Recently accepted papers from other venues are welcome for poster presentation.
  • Reviewing is double-blind and runs on OpenReview.
  • Accepted papers are presented in the poster session, and four are selected for spotlight talks.

Invited speakers

We plan eight invited talks by speakers from computer vision, computer graphics, robotics, and industry. More names will appear here as they are confirmed.

  • Andrea Vedaldi University of Oxford

    Andrea Vedaldi is Professor of Computer Vision and Machine Learning at the University of Oxford, where he co-leads the Visual Geometry Group (VGG) in the Department of Engineering Science. He is also a Research Scientist and Technical Lead at Meta, working on 3D computer vision and generative AI, and an Amazon Scholar in the Frontier AI and Robotics lab. His research develops computer vision and machine learning methods that interpret the semantic content and the 3D geometry of images and videos with minimal human labeling, with a current focus on spatial intelligence. His honors include a Royal Society Faraday Discovery Fellowship, the IEEE Thomas Huang Memorial Prize, the IEEE Mark Everingham Prize, two CVPR Best Paper Awards, and an ACM Test of Time Award. He is a Fellow of the Royal Academy of Engineering and of ELLIS, and has served as a chair of ECCV.

  • Rana Hanocka University of Chicago

    Rana Hanocka is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the University of Chicago and the Founder and CEO of Thrixel. She leads 3DL (threedle!), a group of researchers working on 3D, machine learning, and visual computing. She obtained her Ph.D. in 2021 from Tel Aviv University under the supervision of Daniel Cohen-Or and Raja Giryes. Her research builds artificial intelligence for 3D data, spanning computer graphics, machine learning, and computer vision, with the goal of bringing the success of deep learning on structured data to unstructured 3D geometry. Her group developed MeshCNN, a convolutional neural network designed specifically for meshes, and explored learning from the internal data of a single shape for surface reconstruction, geometric texture synthesis, and point cloud consolidation. Her current interests include intertwining human and machine creativity in 3D modeling and animation, learning with less supervision from large shape collections, and making 3D neural networks more interpretable.

Advisors

  • Richard (Hao) Zhang Simon Fraser University

    Hao (Richard) Zhang is a Distinguished SFU Professor in the School of Computing Science at Simon Fraser University and Vice President of AI and R&D at Augmenta, where he leads the company's effort in building foundation models for construction. He obtained his Ph.D. from the Dynamic Graphics Project at the University of Toronto and his MMath and BMath degrees from the University of Waterloo. He directs the GrUVi (Graphics U Vision) Lab, and his research is in computer graphics and visual computing, with special interests in geometric and generative modeling, shape analysis, 3D vision, spatial AI, geometric deep learning, and computational design and fabrication. He has published more than 200 papers on these topics, including over 80 articles in SIGGRAPH, SIGGRAPH Asia, and ACM Transactions on Graphics. He is an IEEE Fellow, a member of the ACM SIGGRAPH Academy, and was an Amazon Scholar from late 2021 to early 2025. Fourteen of his former students and postdocs are now professors themselves.

  • Jiajun Wu Stanford University

    Jiajun Wu is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science and, by courtesy, of Psychology at Stanford University. Before joining Stanford, he was a Visiting Faculty Researcher at Google Research, working with Noah Snavely. He finished his Ph.D. at MIT, advised by Bill Freeman and Josh Tenenbaum, and his undergraduate degrees at Tsinghua University, working with Zhuowen Tu. His group studies physical scene understanding, building machines that see, reason about, and interact with the physical world, and asks what levels of abstraction AI systems need in their representations and where they come from, drawing inspiration from the physical world itself and from human cognition. Representative projects include Galileo, MarrNet, 4D Roses, the Neuro-Symbolic Concept Learner, and the Scene Language. His group also applies these representations to multimodal perception from visual, acoustic, and tactile signals, visual generation of the 4D physical world, neuro-symbolic visual reasoning, and robotics and embodied AI.

  • Maneesh Agrawala Stanford University

    Maneesh Agrawala is the Forest Baskett Professor of Computer Science and Director of the Brown Institute for Media Innovation at Stanford University. He is also a consulting AI Scientist at Roblox. He works on computer graphics, human-computer interaction, and visualization. His focus is on investigating how cognitive design principles can be used to improve the effectiveness of audio and visual media. The goals of this work are to discover the design principles and then instantiate them in both interactive and automated design tools. His honors include an Okawa Foundation Research Grant (2006), an Alfred P. Sloan Foundation Fellowship (2007), an NSF CAREER Award (2007), a SIGGRAPH Significant New Researcher Award (2008), a MacArthur Foundation Fellowship (2009), an Allen Distinguished Investigator Award (2014), and induction into the SIGCHI Academy (2021). He was named an ACM Fellow in 2022.
